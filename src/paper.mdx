## Abstract

We propose PhysiCalWorld, a framework that calibrates action-conditioned world models to the instance-specific dynamics of novel test scenes via in-context prompting, without requiring test-time fine-tuning or analytical physics modeling. Given an exploratory interaction clip collected within the test scene, our method is able to encode its dynamics into a compact physics descriptor token, which calibrates subsequent predictions to adhere to the scene's actual physical behavior. During training, this descriptor is learned end-to-end through self-supervised video prediction; at inference time, it is extracted once and can be reused across rollouts with negligible computational overhead. We evaluate PhysiCalWorld across four environments featuring diverse physical variations and show that it substantially improves rollout prediction accuracy and enables effective visual planning, closely matching or exceeding privileged baselines that access ground-truth physical parameters.

</HighlightedSection>

The context encoder $g_\phi$ extracts physical dynamics and compresses the sequence into a compact physics descriptor token $\mathbf{z}_{\text{phy}}$. ### Quantitative Evaluation

We evaluate PhysiCalWorld across four challenging benchmark environments with diverse physical property variations. Across all environments, in-context physics calibration consistently improves next-frame visual fidelity, substantially mitigates open-loop rollout drift, and elevates visual planning success rates, matching or exceeding privileged baselines that access ground-truth physical parameters.
